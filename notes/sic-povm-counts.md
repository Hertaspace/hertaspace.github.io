## Statement

SICs exist in every dimension, as the [earlier note](sic-povms-lean.html) records. A finer question is what all of them look like. Almost every known SIC is generated by the Weyl–Heisenberg displacement operators \(D_p\) from a single fiducial vector, so the question becomes: which fiducial vectors are there?

Here the fiducial vector and its complex conjugate are replaced by two independent vectors \(a, b \in \mathbb{C}^d\) with \(S = b^T a \neq 0\). Put \(\Pi = a b^T / S\). The complexified SIC equations are

$$ (d+1)\,\mathrm{tr}(\Pi D_p)\,\mathrm{tr}(\Pi D_p^\dagger) = 1 \qquad \text{for every } p \neq 0. $$

Hermitian solutions, those with \(\Pi = \Pi^\dagger\), are the SIC fiducial projectors. No symmetry, Hermiticity or subspace is assumed. The table counts individual projectors, before any quotient by a symmetry group.

| Dimension | Solutions | Hermitian (SIC fiducials) | Non-Hermitian |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | 512 | 256 | 256 |
| 5 | 4000 | 2000 | 2000 |
| 6 | 6912 | 3456 | 3456 |
| 7 | 32928 | 16464 | 16464 |

- Every solution is a simple point, so these are exact counts with multiplicity one.
- In dimension 7 the Hermitian solutions are exactly the two known extended Clifford orbits, 7a with 10976 projectors and 7b with 5488.
- In dimensions 6 and 7 every solution, Hermitian or not, has ordinary projective Clifford stabilizer exactly a Zauner group of order 3. The strong Zauner conjecture, Appleby's Conjecture A, therefore holds in these two dimensions.
- For \(4 \le d \le 7\) every non-Hermitian solution is a displaced-parity ghost: \(\Pi^\dagger = u \Pi u^{-1}\) for some \(u = D_q P\), where \(P\) is the parity operator. So Khodaiemehr's Conjecture III.1 holds in these four dimensions.

## How it is proved

An upper bound comes from counting all paths of a perturbed system and subtracting those that end elsewhere. In dimension 7 the total is 3784704. At least 1671040 paths end on the boundary and at least 2080736 end at spurious roots, which leaves at most 32928 for the SIC equations. Exact solutions in six Clifford orbits reach this bound, so the count is exact and every point is simple. Dimensions 4, 5 and 6 are closed with exact computations modulo the primes 5, 3 and 7.

The proofs combine finite-field and exact polynomial computations in Singular and msolve, Z3 unsatisfiability checks, and interval arithmetic for the spurious roots, together with written arguments. They are computer-aided and not formalized in a proof assistant. Z3's verdicts are trusted without independently checked proof objects.

## Credit

H. Khodaiemehr counted 512, 4000 and 6912 numerically by homotopy continuation and conjectured the SIC–ghost split ([arXiv:2611.01524](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01524)). The known SIC orbits come from Renes et al. ([arXiv:quant-ph/0310075](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0310075)), Appleby ([arXiv:quant-ph/0412001](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0412001)) and Scott and Grassl ([arXiv:0910.5784](https://arxiv.org/abs/0910.5784)). Ghost existence is due to Appleby, Flammia and Kopp ([arXiv:2609.39192](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39192)). The new part is the certified completeness of the full complexified solution sets, including dimension 7. The computations were carried out by large language model agents under human direction, and agents from different vendors cross-checked parts of them.

## Checks

The [repository](https://github.com/Hertaspace/sic-povm-counts) has step-by-step proof notes, every source program and certificate, and one replay command per claim. A clean replay of every claim in a fresh Linux environment passed on 6 November 2026, the longest taking 81 minutes. Its records are in the repository. Independent checking is welcome.
